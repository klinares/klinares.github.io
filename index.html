About

I am a survey methodologist working at the intersection of measurement science, probability sampling, and applied data science. My research training comes through the University of Maryland Joint Program in Survey Methodology (JPSM) and the University of Michigan Survey Research Center, where I earned an MS in Survey and Data Science; I also hold an MA in Quantitative Psychology.

My work spans probability sample design, latent variable modeling, multilevel and Bayesian methods, and the responsible integration of machine learning and large language models into survey research workflows. My methodological foundation rests on the Total Survey Error (TSE) framework developed by Groves and colleagues, extended through the Total Error Framework (TEF) of Amaya, Biemer, and Kinyon (2020), which adapts TSE to non-probability and Big Data sources such as social media, administrative records, and AI-generated content. This dual foundation lets me reason about data quality consistently across traditional surveys and the alternative data streams that increasingly supplement them.

I am especially interested in questions about measurement validity (how new tools affect what we are actually measuring) and in building reproducible, transparent analytic pipelines that make methodological decisions easy to audit.

Most of what you'll find here is built in R, organized around the tidyverse, and rendered through Quarto and Shiny. I favor functional code, fully reproducible pipelines, and methods documentation that travels with the code.

Latent-to-Language: Psychometric-Informed Prompts for LLM-Based Survey Imputation

Psychometrics · LLM

An R-based research framework exploring how psychometrically-informed prompts can guide large language models in the multiple imputation of missing survey items. The project investigates whether translating measurement-model output into natural-language context produces imputations that better preserve the latent structure of the data than purely algorithmic approaches. For survey methodologists, this opens a path toward LLM-assisted imputation that respects the psychometric properties of the instrument rather than treating items as arbitrary tabular features.

Predicting MMR Vaccine Dropout in Afghan Children

Supervised classification study using the 2023 UNICEF Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS) to predict measles vaccination dropout among Afghan children aged 24+ months. Benchmarked five algorithms (logistic regression, bagging, random forest, neural network, and XGBoost) under a tidymodels pipeline with 10-fold cross-validation, an 80/20 train-test split, and F1-optimized tuning. The neural network achieved the strongest performance (F1 = 0.722, AUC = 0.633), with provincial population density and household characteristics emerging as key predictors. The work shows how complex-survey microdata can be enriched with administrative indicators to produce actionable risk profiles for public health programs operating in low-resource settings.

Detroit Transportation Multi-Stage Survey Design

A three-stage area probability sample design for a Detroit face-to-face transportation survey. Targets 1,000 respondents evenly split across four age domains using a composite Measure of Size that delivers self-weighting domain samples and equal interviewer workload across PSUs. Built on 2020 Decennial Census DHC data for 265 census tracts and 612 block groups, with Madow systematic PPS selection (sampling::UPsystematic), Kish (1965) tract linking, and a paired-strata variance estimator to handle the zero joint inclusion probabilities inherent in systematic PPS. The design illustrates how composite MOS reconciles competing constraints (self-weighting, equal workload, domain precision) in a single coherent specification ready for fielded city-level survey work.

Michigan Teen Smoking & Drug Use Survey Design

A two-stage stratified cluster sample design developed for the Michigan Department of Education to monitor teen smoking and drug use across grades 7–12 statewide. Schools (PSUs) are stratified across nine regions and selected with probability proportional to estimated size (PPeS); students are selected systematically within school. The design jointly satisfies CV ≤ 0.05 across three outcomes and a $500K budget by computing optimal cluster size from pilot-study DEFFs, evaluating three candidate clustering options, and adjusting for 30% school and 70% student response rates, yielding 88 schools and ~4,721 students from a frame of 830,138. The work demonstrates end-to-end design optimization under realistic budget, precision, and response-rate constraints, the kind of integrated planning required for state agency surveys.

05

DOGE Days on Reddit: Public Sentiment in a Federal Shakeup

A two-paper research pipeline analyzing public discourse on the Department of Government Efficiency's federal workforce reductions. Scraped 557 posts and 12,553 comments across 19 subreddits via the Reddit API, hand-coded 400 comments with a four-rater team (54% opposed, 28% favor, 18% neutral), and analyzed activity peaks around major news events. A companion paper applied supervised ML (SVM, KNN) and zero-shot LLM classification (LLaMA 3.2B, Gemma 3.12B) for stance detection. The work is a direct application of the Total Error Framework: it interrogates how non-probability digital trace data can complement probability surveys, and how zero-shot LLMs perform against trained classifiers on attitudinal coding tasks where coverage and measurement error structures differ fundamentally from traditional survey instruments.

06

Player Market Value in Elite European Football

A cross-classified multilevel model of player market value in elite European football, fit to 7,023 player-season observations across 2,159 unique players and 21 top clubs (2013–2024). Treats players and clubs as crossed (not nested) random effects to handle inter-club transfers, with cubic time terms centered on 2020 to isolate the COVID-era market correction. Estimated in lme4 via REML with the BOBYQA optimizer; significance via Satterthwaite approximations. Findings include a peak valuation age of around 27, a 42% premium per SD of within-club playing time, and a 17% domestic-citizenship discount. The cross-classified framework transfers directly to survey contexts where respondents move between clusters over time, such as students changing schools or employees changing firms in panel surveys.

Dog Breed Explorer: A Data-Visualization Dashboard

An interactive Quarto dashboard for exploring 391 AKC dog breeds, scraped from dogTime and structured for visual comparison. The dashboard combines mosaic plots, small-multiples panels, and an interactive 2-to-4-breed comparison tool to reveal behavioral and trait signatures across AKC breed groups. Framed as a simulated data-science presentation to a corporate audience, the project showcases applied design principles (Tufte data-ink ratio, small multiples) alongside reproducible R/Quarto authoring. Effective dissemination is itself a methodological concern in survey research: well-designed dashboards make complex findings legible to non-technical decision-makers without sacrificing analytic rigor.

Open-Source Data Analysis of Civilian Impact in Gaza

A web-scraping and data-visualization project that draws on the Airwars open-source archive to examine the civilian impact of military operations in Gaza. Built end-to-end in R and Quarto, the project handles structured extraction of incident-level records, frame construction, and a series of exploratory visualizations developed for a JPSM data-visualization course. Within the Total Error Framework, the project sits squarely in the territory the framework was designed for: open-source incident archives serving as alternative data when probability sampling of an affected population is infeasible, with the attendant questions of coverage and reporting bias that a survey methodologist is equipped to assess.

UM-IHC Summer 2025: AI in Survey & Health Measurement

Methodological work from a summer 2025 internship at the University of Maryland Institute for Health Computing. Applies Tourangeau's cognitive response process framework (comprehension, retrieval, judgment, response) to evaluate the integration of large language models into patient-reported outcome measures (LLM-PROMs). Identifies validity threats at each cognitive stage and argues that classic survey-methodology theory, in particular the measurement-error component of the Total Survey Error framework, provides the conceptual tools needed to evaluate AI-enhanced measurement. Presented at DC-AAPOR, September 2025.
